A heatmap colours a grid of cells by value. Each row of your data holds a line of cells: its
`values` (an array) fill the cells across `bins`, and its position runs along X, or up the plot
when the series sets `y`. `appendData` adds rows, so a waterfall or a spectrogram scrolls as rows
arrive, and `streaming.maxPoints` keeps the latest.

The waterfall below is a simulated spectrum analyser: ten sweeps a second of a band from 400 to
410 MHz, each a row of 100 bins, newest at the top, thirty seconds kept. A steady carrier, a
drifting one and a transmitter hopping between channels stand above the noise floor.

## Bins

`bins` places the cells across each row, and a cell that is not in the grid is not drawn:

- `{ start, step, count }`: `count` cells, `step` apart, the first centred on `start`.
- `{ centers }`: each cell's centre. A cell reaches half way to its neighbours, and a step more
  than 1.75 times the steps beside it is a gap, so a missing bin leaves a gap rather than
  widening the bins beside it.
- `{ edges }`: the cells' edges, one more than there are cells.

Rows reach half way to their neighbours too. A pause of more than 2.5 typical steps between two
rows is left empty, so a stream that stopped shows where, while a late or an early row does not.

## Rows along X

Without `y`, each row is a column of cells at the chart's `x`: a spectrogram, or a calendar of
hours by day.

## Angles and missing values

An angle, a phase or a time of day wraps around, so it takes `colors: 'cyclic'`, whose ends meet:
355° and 5° get neighbouring colours. A `null`, `undefined` or `NaN` value draws in the empty
colour, not as the lowest value: below, the simulated station's vane froze for six hours.

## 2D histograms

[`histogram2d(x, y)`](/snaplot/reference/api/snaplot/functions/histogram2d/) counts pairs of values
into a grid, which a heatmap takes as its `data`. Pass `bins` for counts per axis, or
`xThresholds` and `yThresholds` for edges of your own. A grid of your own is
`{ x: bins, y: bins, values }`, with one value per cell, row by row from the lowest Y.

## Colours

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `colors` | `'sequential'` | `'sequential'`, `'diverging'` (symmetric about zero) or `'cyclic'` (phases and angles), from the theme; or your own stops, low to high. |
| `domain` | the values' extent | The values at the two ends of the colours. Set it on a stream, so the colours keep their meaning as rows arrive. |
| `clamp` | `true` | Values past the domain take the end colours; `false` leaves them undrawn. Cyclic colours wrap. |
| `emptyColor` | a tint of the theme's muted colour | The colour of a `null`, `undefined` or `NaN` value. |
| `colorScale` | `true` | The colour scale beside the plot, with `title`, `ticks` and `format`. |

The theme's `sequential`, `diverging` and `cyclic` tokens set the ramps, and each has a light and
a dark default. The tooltip and `getCursor()` report the cell under the pointer: `x`, `y` and
`value`, with the cell's `xRange` and `yRange`.
