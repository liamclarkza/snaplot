`type: 'line'` is the default: a series without a `type` is a line through its `y` values in X
order. `lineWidth` sets its width in CSS pixels, and `dash` draws dashes or dots, as `[6, 3]` or
`[2, 2]`.

<Example name="chart-types/line" />

## Steps and curves

`interpolation` shapes the path between points: `'linear'` (the default), `'monotone'` (smooth,
never past a point) and three steps. `'step-after'` holds each value until the next point, the
shape of a meter reading that covers the hour after its time; `'step-before'` and
`'step-middle'` change at the point and halfway to it.

<Example name="chart-types/line-step" />

## Gaps

A missing value (`null`, `undefined` or `NaN`) breaks the line, and a value on its own between
two gaps draws as a dot. `spanGaps: true` bridges the gaps instead. See
[Data](/snaplot/guides/data/#gaps) for what counts as missing.

## Two Y axes

A series names its axis with `yAxis`; any key under `axes` other than `x` is a Y axis, on the
left unless `position: 'right'`. Each axis ranges over its own series.

<Example name="chart-types/line-two-axes" />

## A log axis

A series that falls over decades reads better on `type: 'log'`, which gives each decade the same
height. See [Axes](/snaplot/guides/axes/#axis-types) for the other axis types.
