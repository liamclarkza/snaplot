## Tooltip modes

`tooltip.mode` picks what the tooltip lists:

- **`'index'`** (the default): every series at the x nearest the pointer, anywhere over the plot
  inside the data's x range. The header is the x; each row is the series swatch, name and value,
  in series order.
- **`'nearest'`** (the default when every series is a scatter): the point nearest the pointer,
  within `tooltip.distance` of it (32 px for a mouse, 44 px for touch, unless you set it). Points
  that coincide are all listed.

### How close the pointer has to be

`tooltip.distance` is the reach of `'nearest'`, in CSS pixels: a point farther from the pointer
than that shows no tooltip. Raise it for sparse data, lower it to keep the tooltip from popping up
between points, or pass `Infinity` to show the nearest point wherever the pointer is over the
plot. `'index'` has no reach: it shows wherever the pointer is over the plot's x range.

```ts
// Always show the nearest point while the pointer is over the plot.
chart.update({ tooltip: { mode: 'nearest', distance: Infinity } });
```

With `Infinity`, each pointer move searches the whole line, or the whole cloud's grid for a
scatter, so keep a finite reach on series of millions of points.

Values are written at the precision of the data, through the series `format`, else the axis
`format`. On a bar chart, lines and points on the same chart join the bar's tooltip. The tooltip
sits `tooltip.offset` CSS pixels (12 by default) right of and below the pointer, shows above open
dialogs and fullscreen elements, flips to stay in the viewport, and on touch it sits above the
finger and stays after a tap until you tap elsewhere or scroll.

`tooltip: false` hides it; the crosshair stays unless `cursor: false`.

### The crosshair

`cursor` takes options for the crosshair and the dots it puts on each series under the pointer:

```ts
chart.update({
  cursor: {
    snap: false, // follow the pointer exactly instead of the nearest sample
    indicators: false, // no dots on the series
    xLine: true, // the vertical line (off by default on bar-only charts)
    yLine: true, // a horizontal line at the pointer's height
  },
});
```

`cursor: true` is the default; `cursor: false` hides the crosshair and its dots, and the tooltip
still follows the pointer.

with `textContent`, as here, or escape any text that comes from data before you return a string.

## The legend

With two or more series, or a scatter coloured by category, the chart shows a legend above the
plot (`legend: 'auto'`). `legend: { position: 'bottom' }` moves it below; `true` and `false`
force it on or off. The legend takes its height from the chart, so the plot fits around it at
any size, and it needs no stylesheet.

Each series is a button named by its label. A click shows or hides the series and reports
`onHiddenSeriesChange`; a hover or focus highlights it (`onHighlightChange`) and dims the
others. A hidden series keeps its place in the legend, faded and struck through. A category
legend lists each colour with its category and does not toggle.

## A custom legend

To build your own legend, hide the built-in one (`legend: false`) and drive the chart's state:
`hiddenSeries` and `highlight` are options, with `onHiddenSeriesChange` and `onHighlightChange`,
and `getState().series` lists each series' label, colour, mark and visibility.

### Legend tables

`createLegendTable(element, { chart })` renders a table of each series' value at the cursor,
or at the latest data when the cursor is off the chart. In React and Solid, `<LegendTable>`
does the same. Columns are `'swatch'`, `'name'`, `'value'` or your own
`{ header, value(point, series) }`; a column's value may be a DOM node, such as a button, and
the table keeps it in place while the cursor moves, so focus inside it survives. See the
[React page](/snaplot/frameworks/react/) for an example.
