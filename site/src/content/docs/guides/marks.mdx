A series' `type` picks its mark. Every mark takes `label`, `color`, `opacity`, `xAxis` and
`yAxis`; the table lists what else each reads.

| `type` | Reads | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `'line'` (default) | `y` | `interpolation`, `dash`, `spanGaps`, `lineWidth`. |
| `'area'` | `y` | A line filled to zero. `fill` sets the fill; `fill: null` draws the outline only. |
| `'band'` | `y`, `lower`, `upper` | A range with a centre line at `y`. |
| `'bar'` | `y` | One bar per x value; several bar series share each slot. |
| `'scatter'` | `y` | Points; `colorBy`, `sizeBy`, `tooltipFields`, `renderMode`. |
| `'histogram'` | bins | `data` from [`histogram()`](/snaplot/reference/api/snaplot/functions/histogram/). |

## Line

`interpolation` shapes the path between points: `'linear'`, `'monotone'` (smooth, never past a
point) and the `'step-before'`, `'step-after'` and `'step-middle'` steps. A missing value breaks
the path; `spanGaps: true` bridges it. A value on its own between two gaps draws as a dot.
`dash` draws dashes or dots, as `[6, 3]` or `[2, 2]`.

## Area

An area is a line filled down to zero, in the line's own interpolation. Its fill is a translucent
ramp of its colour; `fill` sets your own, and `fill: null` keeps the outline only.

## Band

A band fills between `lower` and `upper` and draws `y` as its centre line. Give it a muted
`color` when it is context for another series, such as a normal range.

<Example name="marks-guide/band" />

## Bar

Bars sit in slots centred on their x values, so a numeric x of `0, 1, 2…` reads like categories.
Several bar series share each slot side by side. `color` can be a function of the value and
the row, for a colour per bar.

## Scatter

`colorBy` colours points by a field: strings and a few distinct integers become categories with
a legend entry each, other numbers a ramp. `sizeBy` scales the radius by a field. The tooltip of
a point names its x and y, then the colour category, then `tooltipFields` in order.

<Example name="marks-guide/scatter" />

Above 200,000 points in view, `renderMode: 'auto'` (the default) draws a density map instead of
points; `'points'` and `'density'` pin one or the other.

## Histogram

`histogram(values)` bins numbers on nice edges and returns bins a histogram series reads as its
`data`. Pass `{ bins }` for a bin count or a rule (`'sturges'`, `'scott'`), or `{ thresholds }`
for edges of your own, such as another histogram's `edges` to bin two datasets alike.
